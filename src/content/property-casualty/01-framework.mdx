# 1. The framework — how Florida property insurance law is organized

A homeowner's insurance policy is a **contract**. When the insurer does not
pay what the policyholder believes the contract requires, the underlying
dispute is a **breach-of-contract** dispute. Florida layers a detailed
statutory scheme on top of that contract, found mainly in **Chapter 627,
Part X of the Florida Statutes** (sections 627.701–627.7155), plus the
Insurance Code generally (Chapter 624).

**The 2022–2023 reforms changed almost everything.** In response to a wave of
litigation and several insurer insolvencies, the Legislature passed three
major bills in roughly a year:

- **SB 2-D** (signed May 26, 2022)
- **SB 2-A** (signed December 16, 2022) — the landmark property-insurance bill
- **HB 837** (signed March 24, 2023) — broader tort reform

Together these bills **shortened claim deadlines, eliminated one-way
attorney's fees for property cases, prohibited most post-loss assignments of
benefits, added a pre-suit notice requirement, allowed policies to require
binding arbitration, and raised the bar for bad-faith suits.** Much of what
follows reflects the post-reform landscape. A critical consequence: **the date
a policy was issued or renewed can determine which version of the law
applies.** Many of the new rules apply to policies issued or renewed **on or
after December 16, 2022**; older policies may still be governed by the prior,
more generous rules.

**A few concepts that recur throughout a claim:**

- **ACV vs. RCV.** *Actual Cash Value* is replacement cost minus depreciation.
  *Replacement Cost Value* is the cost to repair or replace without a
  depreciation deduction. Many policies pay ACV first and release the
  withheld depreciation ("recoverable depreciation") only after repairs are
  completed and documented.
- **Deductibles.** Florida policies commonly carry a separate, larger
  **hurricane deductible** (often a percentage of the dwelling limit) that
  applies to hurricane losses, and may apply a separate **roof deductible**.
- **Perils and exclusions.** Coverage depends on the specific policy language.
  Common exclusions include wear and tear, lack of maintenance, and gradual
  deterioration.
