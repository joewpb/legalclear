# 2. The two clocks — deadlines that can end a claim

The single most important thing to understand about a Florida property claim
is that **two entirely separate deadlines run at the same time**, and both are
commonly measured **from the date of loss**. They are frequently confused,
including in published summaries. They are not the same.

| The clock | Statute | Length | Runs from | What it controls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Report the claim** | § 627.70132 | **1 year** | date of loss | Deadline to *notify the insurer* of a new or reopened claim. If missed, the claim is barred. |
| **Report a supplemental claim** | § 627.70132 | **18 months** | date of loss | Deadline to notify the insurer of *additional* damage from the same event, after the original claim was adjusted. |
| **File a lawsuit** | § 95.11(2)(e) | **5 years** | date of loss | Statute of limitations to *sue* the insurer for breach of the property insurance contract. If missed, the suit is time-barred. |

**Reading the table:** reporting a claim on time (within 1 year) is a
*separate* obligation from filing suit on time (within 5 years). Doing one
does not satisfy the other. A claim reported promptly can still be lost if
suit is not filed within the limitations period; a suit filed within five
years still fails if the claim was never reported within the reporting window.

**"Date of loss."** For hurricanes, tornadoes, windstorms, severe rain, and
other weather events, § 627.70132(3) fixes the date of loss as the date the
hurricane made landfall or the date the event was verified by the National
Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) — **not** the date damage is
later discovered. For non-weather events (a burst pipe, an appliance failure,
a fire), the date of loss is the date the event occurred.

**Effective-date caveat.** The **1-year / 18-month** reporting deadlines apply
to policies issued or renewed **on or after December 16, 2022**. Losses under
older policies were subject to longer periods (commonly 2 years to report, 3
years for supplemental claims). The **5-year** limitations period predates the
reforms, but SB 2-A changed the point from which it runs to the **date of
loss** (rather than the older "date of breach"). Confirming which policy
period and which deadlines apply to a specific loss is often the first
analytical step in any dispute.

**Tolling.** Limited pauses exist — for example, § 627.70132 tolls the
reporting deadline during a servicemember's deployment to a combat zone.
Filing a mediation request or a Civil Remedy Notice does **not**, by itself,
pause the 5-year limitations clock.
