# 5. Stage three — resolving a dispute without a lawsuit

Florida offers several avenues to resolve a disputed claim short of
litigation. They are not mutually exclusive, and the order in which they are
used can matter.

## 5.1 Department of Financial Services (DFS) mediation — § 627.7015

Florida runs a **free, non-binding, state-administered mediation program** for
residential property claims.

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | An informal settlement conference with a neutral, state-certified mediator. Non-adversarial and confidential. |
| **Who may request** | The policyholder (as a first-party claimant) or the insurer. Third-party assignees are excluded, and an insurer is not required to mediate a request made by an assignee. |
| **Eligibility** | Personal lines and commercial *residential* policies. The amount in controversy must be **at least $500** (unless both sides agree to mediate less). |
| **Not eligible** | Commercial (non-residential) coverage, private-passenger auto, liability coverage, claims the insurer reasonably suspects involve fraud or a material misrepresentation on which the entire claim was denied, and losses that did not comply with § 627.70132. |
| **Cost** | The **insurer bears the cost.** For residential mediation the total is set at **$350** ($300 mediator fee + $50 administrator fee). |
| **How to request** | Through the DFS Division of Consumer Services — online, by the Consumer Helpline (1-877-MY-FL-CFO / 1-877-693-5236), or by mail. The insurer must notify the policyholder of the right to mediate. |
| **Binding?** | No. If mediation does not resolve the dispute, either side may pursue other options. |

A structural point: if the insurer **fails to notify** the policyholder of the
mediation right, or if mediation is requested and its result is rejected, the
policyholder is **not required to submit to the policy's contractual appraisal
process** as a precondition to suing for breach of contract.

## 5.2 Appraisal — a clause in most policies

Many homeowner and commercial residential policies contain an **appraisal
clause**, usually in the "Conditions" section. Appraisal is a form of
alternative dispute resolution with a narrow purpose.

- **What it decides:** the **amount of loss** only — how much the repair or
  replacement costs. It is essentially arbitration limited to valuation.
- **What it does _not_ decide:** **coverage.** If the insurer has *denied* the
  claim entirely (a coverage dispute, not a price dispute), Florida courts
  have held the policyholder generally **cannot be forced into appraisal**,
  because there is no "amount of loss" to appraise when the insurer says it
  owes nothing.
- **How it works (typical four steps):**
  1. Either party makes a **written demand** for appraisal (often required to
     be sent by certified mail; the policy may set a time limit).
  2. Each party selects its own **independent, competent appraiser** (each
     party pays its own).
  3. The two appraisers select a neutral **umpire** (if they cannot agree, a
     court may appoint one). The parties usually split the umpire's cost.
  4. An award **signed by any two of the three** (the two appraisers, or one
     appraiser and the umpire) is **binding as to the amount of loss.**
- **Trade-offs:** appraisal is faster and cheaper than litigation, but an award
  is difficult to overturn (generally only for fraud or a serious procedural
  defect), and electing appraisal typically gives up the right to have a jury
  decide the value.
- **Important interaction with bad faith:** invoking appraisal in a
  residential property claim affects the timing of a later Civil Remedy
  Notice — see Section 10.

## 5.3 A DFS consumer complaint — separate from litigation

Independent of the litigation track, a policyholder may file a complaint with
DFS through its **"Get Insurance Help"** consumer-assistance service. This is
a regulatory-assistance channel; it is not a lawsuit and does not itself
resolve a contract dispute, but it creates a record and sometimes prompts an
insurer to revisit a decision.

## 5.4 Mandatory binding arbitration — § 627.70154

Since the 2022 reforms, insurers are permitted to sell property policies
containing a **mandatory binding arbitration** endorsement, in exchange for a
premium reduction and subject to strict requirements (including a signed
election and the option to buy a policy without the endorsement). If a
specific policy contains such an endorsement, disputes may have to go to
**arbitration instead of court.** Checking the declarations page and
endorsements for an arbitration provision is therefore part of understanding
what options exist.
