# 11. Assignment of Benefits — now prohibited (§ 627.7152)

Historically, policyholders often signed an **Assignment of Benefits (AOB)**,
transferring their claim rights to a contractor or restoration company that
would then deal with (and often sue) the insurer directly. The Legislature
restricted and then eliminated this:

- For residential and commercial property policies **issued on or after
  January 1, 2023**, a policyholder **may not assign, in whole or in part, any
  post-loss insurance benefit.** An attempt to do so is **void, invalid, and
  unenforceable** (§ 627.7152(13)).
- Narrow carve-outs exist (for example, certain assignments to a subsequent
  purchaser of the property, and true powers of attorney), and older policies
  issued before the cutoff may be governed by the prior AOB rules.

The practical effect is that, for current policies, the **policyholder
generally remains the party who must pursue the claim** — a contractor cannot
step into the policyholder's shoes by taking an assignment of benefits. A
"direction to pay," which merely directs where proceeds are sent, is different
from an assignment of the claim itself.
