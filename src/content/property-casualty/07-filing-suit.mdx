# 7. Stage five — filing suit

If the dispute is not resolved, the remaining option is a breach-of-contract
lawsuit against the insurer.

## 7.1 Which court (§ 34.01)

The court is determined by the **amount in controversy** — the principal
amount claimed, excluding interest, costs, and attorney's fees.

| Court | Amount in controversy | Governing rules | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Small Claims** (a county-court division) | **$8,000 or less** | Florida Small Claims Rules (Rule 7.xxx) | Small underpayments; simplified, faster procedure |
| **County Civil** | **$8,000.01 – $50,000** | Florida Rules of Civil Procedure | Mid-size disputes |
| **Circuit Civil** | **More than $50,000** | Florida Rules of Civil Procedure | Large or total losses |

Two notes: (1) a claim cannot be artificially split to stay under a threshold;
and (2) some judicial circuits have entered administrative orders that apply
the **full Florida Rules of Civil Procedure to property insurance cases even
when the amount falls within small-claims jurisdiction.** Checking the local
circuit's practice is part of preparing to file.

## 7.2 The documents (forms)

**Small claims track (Florida Small Claims Rules):**

| Form | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Statement of Claim** (Form 7.330 / 7.331 / 7.332 series, adapted to the claim) | The document that starts the case — the small-claims equivalent of a complaint. If the claim is based on a written document (the policy), a copy must be attached. |
| **Summons / Notice to Appear for Pretrial Conference** (Form 7.322) | Notifies the defendant to appear; issued by the clerk. |
| Pretrial Conference Order and Notice of Trial (Form 7.323) | Issued by the court, not the filer. |

**County or circuit civil track (Florida Rules of Civil Procedure):**

| Form | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Complaint** (Form 1.918 is a general model) | The pleading that states the parties, the facts, the breach, and the relief sought. |
| **Civil Cover Sheet** (Form 1.997) | **Required** for cases over $8,000; reports data to the clerk under § 25.075. |
| **Summons** (Form 1.902) | Commands the defendant to respond; issued by the clerk. |
| Return of service | Proof that the defendant was served. |

**Both tracks:**

| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Application for Determination of Civil Indigent Status** | Requests a waiver of filing and service fees for those who qualify. |
| Florida Courts **E-Filing Portal** account | Self-represented filers may register to file electronically. |

Official, current versions of these forms are published by the Florida Courts
(the state courts' self-help resources) and by county clerks of court.

## 7.3 Serving the insurer

An insurer authorized to do business in Florida is served with process
**through the state's Chief Financial Officer (CFO) as its statutory agent**,
under §§ 48.151 and 624.422. In practice, the summons and complaint (or
statement of claim) are served on the CFO / Department of Financial Services,
which forwards them to the insurer, and a service fee (commonly around $15,
payable to the CFO) applies. The correct party designation on the paperwork
typically names the insurer and states that it is served through the CFO.

## 7.4 Filing fees

Filing fees vary by court level and county and change periodically;
small-claims fees are lower and rise with the amount claimed, and
county/circuit civil fees are higher. Current fee schedules are published by
each clerk of court, and a fee waiver may be available to those who qualify
(Section 7.2).

> **Note on filing deadlines.** A statutory filing deadline that falls on a
> Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday may extend to the next business day under
> Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.514. The deadlines reported in this guide
> are the raw statutory anniversary dates — a weekend or holiday landing point
> may provide additional time.
