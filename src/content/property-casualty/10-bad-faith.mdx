# 10. Bad faith — a separate, later track (§ 624.155 and § 624.1551)

A "bad faith" claim is **not** the same thing as the breach-of-contract suit,
and under current law it generally comes **after** it.

**What it is.** Florida's civil-remedy statute, § 624.155, allows a suit
against an insurer that, among other things, fails to attempt in good faith to
settle a claim when it could and should have. Bad-faith damages can **exceed
the policy limits** and can include consequential losses; attorney's fees are
available; and punitive damages are possible only on a heightened showing (a
general business practice carried out willfully, wantonly, or with reckless
disregard). The statute also clarifies that **mere negligence alone is not bad
faith.**

**The property-specific prerequisite (§ 624.1551).** For property insurers, SB
2-A added a threshold: a bad-faith action under § 624.155(1)(b) may **not
proceed until the insured has first established that the insurer breached the
contract, through an adverse adjudication — a court determination and final
judgment.** Payment of an **appraisal award alone no longer satisfies** this
threshold. In practice this means the breach-of-contract dispute usually must
be **won first**, and only then can a bad-faith action follow. (Florida courts
have held this requirement is substantive and does not apply retroactively to
bad-faith claims that vested before the statute took effect.)

**The Civil Remedy Notice (CRN) — a condition precedent.** Before any
statutory bad-faith suit, the claimant must file a **Civil Remedy Notice of
Insurer Violation** with DFS, on the DFS form, through the DFS portal, and
give the insurer **60 days to cure**.

| CRN feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Form** | Must be on the DFS-provided form and filed through DFS — a letter, email, or phone call to the insurer does not count. |
| **Content** | Must state *with specificity* the statutory provision (and language) violated, the facts and circumstances, the persons involved, and the relevant policy language, and state that it is given to perfect the civil remedy. If DFS returns it for lack of specificity, the 60-day clock does not start until a proper notice is filed. |
| **60-day cure** | If the insurer pays the damages or corrects the violation within 60 days, the statutory bad-faith action is **extinguished** (though the underlying contract rights remain). |
| **Appraisal timing** | In a **residential** property claim, a CRN **may not be filed within 60 days after appraisal is invoked** by any party. |
| **Tolling** | The limitations period for the bad-faith action is tolled for 60 days after the insurer receives the notice, and for 60 days after appraisal is invoked. |

Because the specificity requirements are strict and a defective CRN can defeat
a later bad-faith suit, and because the § 624.1551 sequencing is technical,
bad-faith practice is an area where the assistance of a licensed attorney is
especially significant.
