# 6. Stage four — the mandatory pre-suit gate: Notice of Intent to Initiate Litigation (§ 627.70152)

This is the step most likely to trip up a self-represented person, because
skipping it can get a lawsuit **dismissed** regardless of the merits.

Florida law makes a **written Notice of Intent to Initiate Litigation a
_condition precedent_ to filing suit** under a residential or commercial
property insurance policy. It is filed with the **Department of Financial
Services**, on a DFS-provided form, through the DFS online portal (the
"Property Insurance Intent to Initiate Litigation" system). DFS then transmits
the notice to the insurer.

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **When** | At least **10 business days before filing suit**, and **not before** the insurer has made a coverage determination under § 627.70131. The notice must be served within the § 95.11 limitations period; serving it **tolls** that period for 10 business days if it would otherwise expire during the notice window. |
| **What it must state** | That the notice is given under § 627.70152; the alleged acts or omissions of the insurer (which may include a denial of coverage); and, if a representative files it, that a copy was given to the claimant. |
| **If the claim was denied** | An **estimate of the damages**, if known, may be included. |
| **If coverage was provided** (not a denial) | A **presuit settlement demand** stating the amount claimed (and, where applicable, itemizing damages, fees, and costs) and the **"disputed amount"** (the difference between the claimant's demand and the insurer's offer). |
| **Public record** | Information submitted with the notice is a public record and is displayed on the DFS website. |

**The insurer's response.** The insurer must respond in writing **within 10
business days**:

- If the notice alleges a **denial of coverage**, the insurer may accept
  coverage, continue to deny, or assert a right to reinspect. If it continues
  to deny, the claimant may sue without further notice.
- If the notice alleges something **other than a denial** (for example,
  underpayment), the insurer must make a settlement offer **or** require the
  claimant to participate in appraisal or another form of ADR. If that ADR is
  not concluded **within 90 days** after the 10-day notice period expires, the
  claimant may then file suit without additional notice.

Because a court is directed to **dismiss** a suit filed without complying with
this section, confirming that the notice was properly filed and that the
response window has run is a prerequisite to litigation.
