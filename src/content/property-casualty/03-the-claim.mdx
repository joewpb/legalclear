# 3. Stage one — after the loss: the claim

## 3.1 First notice of loss

A claim begins when the policyholder notifies the insurer of the loss — the
"first notice of loss." Policies universally require **prompt notice**, and a
delay that prejudices the insurer's investigation can give the insurer a
defense even inside the statutory reporting window. Notice is typically given
by phone, through the insurer's app or website, or in writing, and generates
a claim number.

## 3.2 The Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights (§ 627.7142)

Florida requires residential property insurers to send policyholders a
**Homeowner Claims Bill of Rights** within 14 days of receiving a first notice
of loss. It is a plain-language summary of claim-handling timelines and
responsibilities. It does **not** by itself create a separate right to sue,
but it usefully collects the deadlines below.

## 3.3 What the insurer must do, and by when (§ 627.70131)

For residential claims, the Insurer's duty statute imposes these timelines
(as amended effective March 1, 2023):

| Insurer's duty | Deadline | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Review and acknowledge a claim communication | **7 calendar days** | § 627.70131(1)(a) |
| Begin a reasonable investigation after receiving proof-of-loss statements | **7 days** | § 627.70131(3)(a) |
| Conduct any physical inspection of the property | **30 days** after proof-of-loss statements | § 627.70131(3)(b) |
| Provide the adjuster's name and state license number | with the inspection / communications | § 627.70131(3)(b)–(c) |
| Send a copy of the insurer's detailed estimate of the loss | **7 days** after it is generated (on request) | § 627.70131 |
| **Pay or deny the claim, or pay the undisputed portion** | **60 days** after notice of the claim | § 627.70131(7)(a) |
| Pay interest if the deadline is missed | interest accrues **from the date the insurer received notice** of the claim, at the § 55.03 rate | § 627.70131(7)(a) |

Two practical notes: (1) the 60-day pay-or-deny period can be extended for
factors beyond the insurer's control, and the Office of Insurance Regulation
can grant additional time after a declared state of emergency; and (2) courts
have held that a violation of these timelines does not, standing alone, create
a private cause of action — the remedy generally runs through breach of
contract and, in appropriate cases, bad faith.

## 3.4 What the policyholder is expected to do (post-loss conditions)

Most policies impose "post-loss obligations" as conditions of coverage.
Failure to meet them can jeopardize a claim independent of any deadline.
Commonly, a policy requires the policyholder to:

- **Give prompt notice** of the loss.
- **Mitigate** — take reasonable emergency measures to prevent further damage
  (for example, tarping a roof or stopping a water source), and keep receipts
  for those measures.
- **Protect and preserve** the damaged property, and, where feasible, **keep**
  damaged items for inspection rather than discarding them.
- **Document** the loss with photographs or video taken before and after any
  emergency repairs, and maintain an inventory of damaged property.
- **Submit a sworn proof of loss** if the policy or the insurer requests one,
  usually within a stated number of days.
- **Cooperate** with the investigation, produce requested records, and
  **submit to an examination under oath (EUO)** if the insurer invokes that
  policy right.
- **Allow inspection** of the property by the insurer's adjuster.

## 3.5 A claim-file checklist

Keeping an organized record from day one materially affects how a dispute
unfolds. A useful set of materials to preserve includes: the full policy
(including the declarations page and all endorsements); the claim number and
every communication with the insurer; dated photographs and video of the
damage; an itemized inventory of damaged property with values; receipts for
emergency repairs, temporary housing, and out-of-pocket expenses; any
independent estimates obtained from licensed contractors; and the insurer's
estimate, payment records, and any denial or coverage letters.
