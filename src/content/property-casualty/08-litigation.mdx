# 8. Stage six — the litigation lifecycle

What happens after filing depends on which track the case is on.

## 8.1 Small-claims track

1. **Filing** of the Statement of Claim and issuance of the Summons/Notice to
   Appear.
2. **Service** on the insurer through the CFO.
3. A **pretrial conference** — the first court date. This is typically where
   the court refers the parties to **mediation**.
4. **Court-connected mediation.** Many small-claims disputes settle here.
5. If unresolved, **trial before a judge** (small-claims trials are bench
   trials unless a jury is demanded and available). The Small Claims Rules are
   streamlined, and the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure apply only to the
   extent invoked.
6. **Judgment.** A losing party generally has 10 days to move for a new trial
   and 30 days to appeal.

## 8.2 County / circuit civil track

1. **Complaint** filed with the Civil Cover Sheet; **Summons** issued and
   served through the CFO.
2. The insurer files an **Answer** (generally due **20 days** after service)
   raising defenses, and may assert a counterclaim.
3. **Discovery** — the formal exchange of information: interrogatories
   (written questions), requests for production (documents), requests for
   admission, and depositions (sworn testimony).
4. **Mediation** — Florida courts routinely order civil cases to mediation
   before trial.
5. **Motion practice** — including a possible **motion for summary judgment**,
   which asks the court to decide the case (or an issue) without trial when
   there is no genuine dispute of material fact.
6. **Trial** — before a judge or, if demanded, a jury.
7. **Judgment** and any post-judgment motions or appeal.

At every stage in the civil track, the self-represented party is responsible
for meeting deadlines, serving documents, conducting discovery, and complying
with the Rules of Civil Procedure and any local orders.
