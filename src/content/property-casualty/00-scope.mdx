# 0. Scope — what this guide covers

Florida "property and casualty" insurance is a broad category. This guide
covers the one type of dispute a self-represented person is most likely to
pursue: a **first-party residential property insurance claim** — that is, a
claim a homeowner, condominium unit owner, or renter makes **under their own
policy**, against **their own insurer**, for physical damage to the insured
property. Typical losses include hurricane and windstorm damage, water damage
from a burst pipe or roof leak, fire, and theft.

**This guide does _not_ cover the following, which run on different legal tracks:**

| Not covered here | Why it's different | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Auto insurance / PIP | Governed by the Florida Motor Vehicle No-Fault Law and separate statutes | Auto module |
| Third-party liability claims | A claim against *someone else's* insurer for injury or damage they caused | Liability module |
| Commercial (non-residential) property | Different statutory timelines and, often, mandatory arbitration | Commercial module |
| Flood damage | Almost always covered by federal NFIP policies, not state law | Federal flood resource |
| Health, life, disability | Entirely separate bodies of law | Other modules |

A single event can involve more than one track (for example, a hurricane that
damages a home *and* a car). Each claim follows its own rules.
